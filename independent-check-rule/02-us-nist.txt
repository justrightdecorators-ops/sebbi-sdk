To the NIST AI team

I am writing on behalf of sebbi.pro (Monop Content). NIST's work on AI standards, and on standards for AI agents in particular, will shape what a sufficient record of an AI system's actions looks like. We ask that it distinguish two kinds of log: one whose integrity only its holder can vouch for, and one that anyone can check. NIST's own secure hash standards are what make the second kind practical. The distinction is set out below as a short, method-neutral rule that we have also put to rule-makers on evidence.

THE INDEPENDENT CHECK RULE (ICR-1)

1. A self-attested record is a record whose integrity can be shown only by the person relying on it, or by someone acting for them.
2. An independently checkable record is a record for which a person with no access to the holder's systems, and no need to trust the holder, can detect any later alteration, deletion or back-dating, using a published method.
3. No presumption of integrity applies to a self-attested record. The person relying on it must show that it has not been altered since it was made.
4. An independently checkable record that passes its check is presumed unaltered since the time of its independent timestamp, unless the contrary is shown.
5. The rule is neutral as to method. Any published method that meets the second clause qualifies.

The rule is about whether a record was changed after it was made. Whether the system that made the record was working correctly is a separate question.

WHY NOW
Logs and records produced by software, and increasingly by AI systems acting on their own, are relied on as evidence of what happened. Most can be altered, deleted or back-dated by the organisation that holds them, with nothing outside that organisation recording the change. For thirty years there was no practical alternative. There is now: methods that let an outsider detect any change have been published since 1991, are in production use, and cost nothing to check. Only a fingerprint leaves the organisation, never the record.
