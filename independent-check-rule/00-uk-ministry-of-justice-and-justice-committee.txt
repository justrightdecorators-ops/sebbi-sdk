THE INDEPENDENT CHECK RULE (ICR-1)

1. A self-attested record is a record whose integrity can be shown only by the person relying on it, or by someone acting for them.
2. An independently checkable record is a record for which a person with no access to the holder's systems, and no need to trust the holder, can detect any later alteration, deletion or back-dating, using a published method.
3. No presumption of integrity applies to a self-attested record. The person relying on it must show that it has not been altered since it was made.
4. An independently checkable record that passes its check is presumed unaltered since the time of its independent timestamp, unless the contrary is shown.
5. The rule is neutral as to method. Any published method that meets the second clause qualifies.

The rule is about whether a record was changed after it was made. Whether the system that made the record was working correctly is a separate question, and the rule leaves the court's time free for it.

THE PROBLEM
In criminal proceedings in England and Wales a computer is presumed to have been working correctly unless the contrary is shown. Section 69 of the Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984, which put the burden the other way, was repealed by section 60 of the Youth Justice and Criminal Evidence Act 1999. The Ministry of Justice called for evidence on the presumption in January 2025. In February and March 2026 peers moved amendments to the Victims and Courts Bill to remove it. The difficulty on every side has been the same: nobody wants trials slowed by proof that every device works, and nobody has offered a test a court can apply quickly.

ONE PART OF THE PROBLEM NOW HAS A CLEAN ANSWER
Two different questions are bundled into 'is this computer evidence reliable'. The first is whether the system that produced the record was working correctly. The second is whether the record has been changed since it was made. The second question no longer needs argument or expert evidence. A record can be kept so that any change, deletion or back-dating is detectable by a person outside the organisation, at no cost to check.

THE PROPOSAL
Adopt the Independent Check Rule (ICR-1) for records produced by software: no presumption of integrity for a self-attested record, and a presumption of integrity for a record that passes an independent check. The rule is neutral as to method and names no product. It keeps uncontroversial evidence moving, because an independently checkable record is confirmed in seconds, and it ends reliance on records that only their owner can vouch for.

THE LAW ALREADY DOES THIS ELSEWHERE
Article 41(2) of Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 (eIDAS) gives a qualified electronic time stamp a presumption of the accuracy of the date and time and of the integrity of the data it is bound to. Security law already measures conduct against the state of the art: Article 32 of the GDPR and UK GDPR requires appropriate measures having regard to the state of the art and the cost of implementation. Article 12 of the EU AI Act requires automatic logs for high-risk AI systems; the standards that will say what a sufficient log is are being written now.

THE EVIDENCE
The State of the Art Notice published with this submission lists the methods, from 1991 to today, and is itself timestamped so its date cannot be disputed. The Editable Log Test lets any person change one line of a log on their own phone and see that nothing outside the organisation records the change, then see the same change caught to the line. Both are free and public.

WHAT WE ASK
That the Independent Check Rule be considered in the Ministry of Justice's work on evidence generated by software, in rules of court on the reliability of computer evidence, and in guidance on the weight to be given to logs in regulatory and civil proceedings. We will give evidence, in writing or in person, and will demonstrate the test.
